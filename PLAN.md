# Plan

Working time is about 5 hours a week. We start with a small budget and look for research credits or funding after milestone 4 or 8.

## Goal

1. Understand the problem the paper studies.
2. Rebuild ConstraintBench and check whether its findings hold.
3. Improve the method.
4. Extend it with new experiments.

## Findings to replicate

The paper tested 6 models on 200 tasks in 10 domains and found:

1. **Feasibility, not optimality, is the bottleneck.** The best model was feasible on 65.0% of tasks, and no model was feasible *and* optimal on more than 30.5%.
2. **Feasible answers are close to optimal** (average objective quality of 89–96%).
3. **Difficulty depends strongly on the domain.** Average feasibility ranges from 85.0% on facility location to 0.8% on crew assignment.
4. **Four failure types:** structural misunderstanding, entity hallucination, constraint-specific mistakes, and budget/capacity violations.

## Scope

We start with 4 domains: one per failure type, from easiest to hardest. We'll add the other 6 later if the results justify it.

| Domain | Paper: avg feasibility | Paper: avg feasible + optimal | Failure type |
|---|---|---|---|

| Shift scheduling | 70.8% | 42.5% | Constraint-specific mistakes |
| Bin packing | 26.7% | 14.2% | Entity hallucination |
| Crew assignment | 0.8% | 0.0% | Structural misunderstanding |

## Improvements over the paper

- **Several attempts per problem, with confidence intervals.** The paper used 1 attempt on 20 problems per domain. At that size, a 50% score has a 95% interval of about ±22 points.
- **Problems generated by seeded code**, with an LLM only writing the scenario text. This is cheaper and reproducible, and it lets us control problem size.
- **Public benchmark and code.** The original was not released as of October 2026.
